# Restaurant_Markeet_Analysis
This report summarizes the data analysis conducted on the Mexico Restaurant Rating dataset. As
a Data Analyst, the goal was to evaluate consumer behavior, restaurant performance, and
market opportunities to guide investment decisions.


## Executive Summary
The analysis of the 2012 Mexico restaurant survey reveals a market heavily influenced by a
young, student-dominated demographic. While Mexican cuisine remains the primary demand,
there are significant underserved niches in the "Coffee Shop" and "Family" dining categories. For
investors, the most successful model is a Medium-priced, indoor establishment that provides
dedicated parking and alcohol service.

## Consumer Profile
The dataset is characterized by a strong Selection Bias, which must be considered when
interpreting results.
Occupation: Over 70% of the consumers are Students.
Age: The demographic is concentrated in the 21–25 age range.
Budget: Most consumers have a Medium or Low budget.
Status: The majority are Single and use Public Transport or walk.
Bias Impact: This data perfectly represents the "College Town" economy but may not reflect the
preferences of high-income professionals or large families.

## Insights from Highest Rated Restaurants
1. Food is the Foundation: A "2" in Food Rating is the strongest predictor of a "2" in Overall
Rating.
2. Service is secondary: consumers will tolerate average service for excellent food, but
not vice versa.
3. There is a direct correlation between Consumer Preference and Rating. When a restaurant
serves the specific cuisine a consumer prefers (e.g., a Mexican-food lover eating at a
Mexican restaurant), the likelihood of a top rating increases by 20%+.
4. Interestingly, High price doe not guarantee a high rating. In this student-heavy sample,
Medium-priced restaurants often score higher because they provide a better value-for-
money experience.

## Demand and Supply Gaps
The Coffee Shop Gap: In San Luis Potosi, there is high demand for Coffee Shops and Cafeterias
from the student population, but only one official location is listed.
The Family Dining Gap: There is a recorded preference for Family-style restaurants, yet many
cities have zero supply in this category.
Niche Fast Food: Hot Dogs and Burgers show consistent demand but lack dedicated, high-rated
specialized venues.
Mexican Cuisine: While supply is high, the demand is so massive that the market can still absorb
more Specialized or Regional Mexican concepts.

## Investment Recommendation

If investing in a new venture, the following characteristics are statistically linked to the highest
consumer satisfaction:

Price Point (Medium) : Aligns with student budgets while allowing for quality.
Cuisine (Coffee Shop / Mexican) : Targets the largest demand and the clearest supply gaps.
Location (San Luis Potosi) : The most active and densest consumer hub in the data.
Environment (Indoor) : Closed areas consistently outscore open-air/street stalls.
Amenities (Valet/Private Parking) : One of the highest-rated features among successful venues.
Service (Wine and Beer License) : Venues with alcohol service receive higher overall ratings than those without.

## Conclusion
The most profitable and safest investment is a Medium-priced Mexican Bistro or a Modern Coffee Shop located in San Luis Potosi. To ensure success, the establishment should be indoors,
offer parking, and provide a Satisfactory to Highly Satisfactory service level, as the student
